# A note must stand on its own

A permanent note should make sense to a reader who lands on it from a search result with nothing else open — and that reader is usually me, two years later, having forgotten everything around it.

That means the note restates the key definitions and the one or two premises its claim depends on, even when this makes it noticeably longer. Pushing that context out into links does not work well in practice: links get renamed, the target notes drift as I revise them, and a reader following five links to reconstruct one claim will simply give up.

Brevity is not the goal; intelligibility in isolation is. A three-line note that only makes sense after reading four others is a fragment of a note, not a note. When I have to choose, I would rather have a note that is a bit long and fully understandable than one that is short and cryptic.

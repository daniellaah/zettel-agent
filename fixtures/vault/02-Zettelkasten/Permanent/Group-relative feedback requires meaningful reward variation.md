# Group-relative feedback requires meaningful reward variation

Subtracting a group's mean reward identifies responses that perform better or worse than their peers. If every sampled response receives the same reward, that group provides no relative ordering signal, and its standard deviation also needs numerical handling. This makes prompt difficulty and verifier discrimination relevant to training. Group comparison cannot manufacture useful preference evidence from identical observed rewards.

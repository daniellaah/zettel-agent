# At-least-one success and repeated reliability

The article distinguishes the probability of at least one successful attempt among k trials from the probability that every one of those trials succeeds. More attempts can improve the former while making the latter requirement harder. It discusses coding and customer-facing workflows as examples where these measures answer different questions. At one trial the measures coincide, while larger trial counts reveal the difference between finding a success and delivering it consistently.

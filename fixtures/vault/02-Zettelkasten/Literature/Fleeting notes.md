# Fleeting notes

Fleeting notes briefly preserve thoughts that arise while attention is occupied elsewhere. Their purpose is to remind the writer of an idea until it can be considered more carefully. They can be captured in whatever form is immediately available, without interrupting the activity to polish the wording or decide how the idea belongs in the larger system. Ahrens recommends collecting these reminders in one inbox for later processing.

Because a fleeting note depends on context still held in memory, it needs to be reviewed soon, ordinarily within a day or two. During review, the writer considers whether the idea contributes to ongoing thinking and develops useful material into a more complete note. Once processed, the reminder can be discarded. Merely accumulating captures does not create an enduring body of usable ideas: a delayed review may leave the writer unable to recover what a fragment meant.

Underlining a passage or writing a brief margin comment can serve the same temporary function when reading. These marks need subsequent work if they are to support later thinking or writing. The capture step is optional when an idea is already clear and there is time to express it directly as a main note.

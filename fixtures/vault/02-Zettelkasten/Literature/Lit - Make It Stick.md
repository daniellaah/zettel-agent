# Lit - Make It Stick

**Source**: Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III, Mark A. McDaniel, *Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning*, 2014

## Main claim
The study habits most people rely on — rereading, highlighting, massed practice — feel effective and mostly are not. The strategies that work better tend to feel harder and slower, so learners systematically choose the wrong ones.

## Techniques the book recommends
### Retrieval practice
Quiz yourself instead of rereading. Recalling strengthens memory and also shows you what you do not know.

### Spacing
Spread practice out and let some forgetting happen between sessions. Apps built on spaced repetition automate this (中文一般叫间隔重复).

### Interleaving
Mix different kinds of problems within one session so that you have to identify the problem type each time.

### Elaboration and generation
Connect new material to what you already know, and try to answer before being shown the answer.

### Calibration
Use objective checks, not your feeling of knowing, to judge how well you have learned something.

## Illusions of knowing
A recurring theme is that ease of processing is misread as learning. Familiar text feels understood; a fluent lecture feels clear. The book argues that learners need external tests precisely because their own judgment is biased toward easy methods.

## My take
Very practical and well sourced, somewhat repetitive. Most useful for me as a checklist for designing a review routine.

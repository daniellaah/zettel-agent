# Literature notes

Literature notes preserve selected content from reading in the reader’s own words. Ahrens recommends recording what seems worth remembering or potentially useful for later thinking and writing, rather than attempting to retain everything in a text. Quotations should be used selectively, because copying a passage can avoid the work of understanding it. The reading record stays with the bibliographic information in the reference system and remains connected to the source’s context.

Rephrasing should give an accurate account of the original meaning. An idea has a role within its author’s argument, theory or perspective, and that role must not be changed merely to make it fit the reader’s purposes. Even unchanged quotations can misrepresent a text when removed from the context that gives them meaning.

The amount of explanation depends on the text, its difficulty, the reader’s familiarity and the intended use. Keywords may suffice for familiar material, while an unfamiliar argument may require a fuller account. Literature notes help the reader understand the text and prepare material for developing main notes. They should remain directed toward that purpose rather than become an extensive project of their own.

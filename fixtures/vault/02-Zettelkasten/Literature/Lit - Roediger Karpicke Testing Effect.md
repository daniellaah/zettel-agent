# Lit - Roediger Karpicke Testing Effect

**Source**: Henry L. Roediger III & Jeffrey D. Karpicke, "Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention", *Psychological Science*, 2006

## Setup
Students studied short prose passages. Afterwards some groups studied the passage again, while others took a free-recall test on it without feedback. Retention was then measured after different delays, from a few minutes up to a week.

## Findings
- At the shortest delay, the restudy groups did slightly better.
- At the longer delays (two days, one week), the groups that had been tested recalled clearly more than the groups that had restudied, and the gap grew with the delay.
- A follow-up condition with repeated studying versus repeated testing showed the same pattern, more strongly.
- Students who had restudied predicted they would remember more than the tested students predicted — the opposite of what happened.

## Why it matters to me
Two lessons. First, the benefit of testing only shows up after a delay, so a same-day check is misleading. Second, learners' own predictions point the wrong way, which is a concrete example of the fluency problem.

## Limits
Educational materials were short prose; transfer to complex skills needs other evidence. No feedback was given, which makes the testing advantage more impressive rather than less.

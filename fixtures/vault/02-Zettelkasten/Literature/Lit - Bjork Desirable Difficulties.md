# Lit - Bjork Desirable Difficulties

**Source**: Robert A. Bjork, "Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of human beings", 1994; plus the Bjork & Bjork "new theory of disuse" (1992) that underlies it.

## Two kinds of strength
The framework separates how well something is stored from how easily it can be accessed right now. Storage strength only grows. Retrieval strength rises and falls with recency and cues. Crucially, gains in storage strength are larger when retrieval strength is low at the time of practice — that is, when recalling is effortful.

## Why this produces "desirable" difficulties
If effortful access builds more durable learning, then conditions that make access harder during training can pay off later. The paper lists several: varying the conditions of practice, spacing sessions apart, interleaving topics, and using tests rather than re-presentation.

## Performance is not learning
Performance during training reflects current retrieval strength and can be a poor guide to long-term learning. Trainers and learners who optimize for smooth training performance end up choosing massed, blocked, predictable practice.

## Metamemory
People judge their own learning by how fluent the material feels. Since fluency tracks retrieval strength, these judgments are biased in favor of the less effective conditions.

## Caveat
A difficulty is only desirable if the learner can respond to it successfully, possibly with feedback. Arbitrary obstacles do not help.
